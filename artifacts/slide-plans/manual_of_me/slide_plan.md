### Slide 3: Communication Rules & Channels — How We Connect
* **Slide Number:** 3
* **Title:** How We Connect — Communication Preferences & Rules
* **Objective / Purpose:** Provide clear channel routing rules and communication frameworks to streamline interactions and respect cognitive bandwidth.
* **Main Content Points:**
  * **Preferred Channels Matrix:**
    * 💬 **Slack / Teams:** Non-mentally taxing & quick info exchange.
    * 🗣️ **Face-to-Face / Call:** Complex, high-context, or easily misunderstood topics.
    * 📧 **Email:** Official decisions, formal documentation, or 3rd-party communications.
  * **Communication Rules:**
    * **Context Upfront:** Provide background context first unless mutual understanding is already established.
    * **Single-Threading:** Handle only one live F2F or call conversation at a time.
  * **Standard 3-Step Communication Structure:**
    1. Context (facts only)
    2. Problem definition
    3. Proposed solutions (if any)
* **Recommended Layout & Visuals:** Split layout — Left 60% channel matrix rows; Right 40% accent container with step chevron arrows ($1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3$) for the 3-step format.
* **Presenter Notes:** Highlight the 3-step format as the single best way to get a fast, high-quality response.

### Slide 4: Receiving Feedback — The Feedback Guide
* **Slide Number:** 4
* **Title:** Receiving Feedback — The 3-Point Feedback Structure
* **Objective / Purpose:** Establish psychological safety around feedback and teach teammates how to deliver feedback to Huy for maximum impact.
* **Main Content Points:**
  * **Preferred Channel:** Face-to-Face (F2F) — open to both private check-ins and public settings.
  * **3-Point Feedback Structure:**
    1. **Context:** Describe the specific situation/event.
    2. **Shared Understanding of Impact:** Establish alignment on how the issue impacts the business, product, or team.
    3. **Proposed Solution / Improvement (Optional):** Offer actionable ideas or paths forward.
  * **Core Takeaway:** Open to constructive feedback anytime; connecting to business/product/team impact drives immediate action.
* **Recommended Layout & Visuals:** Top F2F open door banner + 3 sequential cards for the 3-point structure.
* **Presenter Notes:** Reassure the audience that feedback is welcomed in any setting (public or private) as long as it's delivered F2F and anchored on impact.

### Slide 5: Work Style & Quirks — Inside the Engine
* **Slide Number:** 5
* **Title:** Inside the Engine — Default Behaviors & Quirks
* **Objective / Purpose:** Demystify Huy's natural working habits so team members can interpret his behaviors accurately during day-to-day work.
* **Main Content Points:**
  * **4 Default Behaviors:**
    1. 💡 **Out-Loud Brainstorming:** Jumps between ideas out loud before focusing on the final solution.
    2. 🧩 **Coherence-Driven:** Easily irritated by incoherence; needs to sort out logical gaps before moving forward.
    3. ⚡ **Topic-Selective Energy:** High energy on Product & Engineering; lower energy in administrative/other areas.
    4. 🚀 **Build & Discover:** Loves to build and run discovery at the same time rather than waiting for fully baked ideas.
* **Recommended Layout & Visuals:** 2x2 Grid with distinct rounded cards for each quirk and a micro-tag labeled *"What to expect"*.
* **Presenter Notes:** Frame these quirks with levity. Clarify that early brainstorming ideas are exploratory, not set in stone, and resolving incoherence is just a logic processing check.

### Slide 6: Debugging & Support Strategies — Debugging Huy
* **Slide Number:** 6
* **Title:** Debugging Huy — Known Issues & Support Plan
* **Objective / Purpose:** Transparently share known personal "bugs" and empower teammates with concrete support actions to resolve friction quickly.
* **Main Content Points:**
  * **4 Known Bugs & Support Actions:**
    1. 🐛 **Bug:** Asks too many questions before committing when a problem isn't fully understood.
       * 🛠️ **Support:** Provide clear context upfront; explain direct relevance to business, product, or team.
    2. 🐛 **Bug:** Responds slowly when running on low energy.
       * 🛠️ **Support:** Give time to recover; explicitly tag/flag if urgent.
    3. 🐛 **Bug:** Tends to be incoherent when over-excited.
       * 🛠️ **Support:** Allow time for things to sink in; echo back what you hear to anchor alignment.
    4. 🐛 **Bug:** Pushes self and others too hard.
       * 🛠️ **Support:** Speak up if it's unsustainable; explain if energy is better spent elsewhere.
* **Recommended Layout & Visuals:** 4 Horizontal Split Rows formatted as `[Known Bug]` $\rightarrow$ `[Support Action]` with terminal/code debug aesthetic styling.
* **Presenter Notes:** Present with humility and humor. Grant explicit permission to teammates to use these support strategies when needed.

### Slide 7: Collaboration Golden Rules — Rules of Engagement
* **Slide Number:** 7
* **Title:** Rules of Engagement — Pet Peeves & Golden Rules
* **Objective / Purpose:** Set clear boundaries, eliminate friction points, and summarize non-negotiables for smooth collaboration.
* **Main Content Points:**
  * **Pet Peeves (What to Avoid):**
    * 🚫 Unscheduled synchronous communication for non-urgent matters.
    * 🚫 Refusal of ideas or proposals without clear reasons.
  * **My Golden Rules (How to Succeed):**
    * 🌟 **Rule 1:** *Notify me beforehand* for synchronous check-ins.
    * 🌟 **Rule 2:** *Always give clear reasons* when refusing an idea/proposal, and optionally suggest alternative solutions.
* **Recommended Layout & Visuals:** Side-by-side contrasting containers (Amber/Red Warning for Pet Peeves vs. Green/Gold Success for Golden Rules).
* **Presenter Notes:** Explain that these rules protect deep focus work and respect everyone's time.
